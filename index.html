Placed, not just transcribed

Field recordings carry where and when — mapped to the site, queued for review, and auditable. A record of the walk, not just the words.

Consent, handled deliberately. Recording laws differ by state and by country — several, including California, require every participant's consent rather than only yours. The platform provides consent prompts and participant notice at the point of recording. Determining and obtaining the consent your jurisdiction requires remains the customer's responsibility. Before & After

What changes when the busywork disappears.

Six moments from six very different businesses. Same platform, shaped six different ways.

Construction

Before: you finish a site walk, then spend the evening reconstructing it — photos in one app, notes in another, the daily log half-done. After: you narrate the walk as it happens. By the time you're back in the truck, a clean daily record is filed to the job and waiting for your sign-off. Your evening is yours again.

Law Firm

Before: a forty-minute client call becomes a sticky note, a half-remembered deadline, and unbilled time. After: the call becomes part of the matter file — summary in place, deadlines on the calendar, every minute captured. The file reads like someone kept perfect notes. Something did.

Event Production

Before: an inquiry sits in your inbox while you rebuild the same proposal, vendor list, and timeline from scratch — again. After: the inquiry becomes a fully staged production plan: client record, draft budget, vendor holds to confirm, run-of-show template. You spend the afternoon on the creative, not the scaffolding.

Membership Club

Before: a member's dues live in a spreadsheet, their bookings in a calendar, their complaint in someone's inbox. Nobody sees the whole person. After: one member record holds dues, bookings, communications, and history. When they call, whoever answers knows the full story — and renewals never slip through.

Program Management

Before: your program status is a Friday scramble — chasing updates across five tools, pasting them into a deck that's stale by Monday. After: workstreams update as work happens, risks surface themselves, and the program picture is live, not assembled. Your status meeting becomes a decision meeting.

Logistics

Before: a driver's check-in call is a scribbled note; the proof of delivery is somewhere; the invoice waits on both. After: the check-in lands on the shipment record, the paperwork files itself, and billing starts the same day the truck is unloaded. Cash stops waiting on paperwork.

FAQ

Questions a careful operator asks.

Will my team actually use it?

That's the design test we hold ourselves to. The workspace uses your terminology and mirrors how your team already works, so day one feels familiar rather than foreign. And because the AI drafts the tedious parts — logs, updates, follow-ups — the system gives time back instead of demanding it. Tools get adopted when they're easier than the old way. That's the bar.

Can clients, subs, and vendors work in it without buying seats?

Yes. External people participate by emailed link — reviewing documents, joining meetings, signing, responding — with no account to create and no license to buy. You pay for your team; the people around your business are free, always.

Can it run multiple companies or brands under one roof?

Yes, natively. Parent companies, subsidiaries, divisions, and client organizations each get their own workspace under their own brand — down to the domain — while leadership holds a view across all of them. If your org chart has layers, the platform mirrors them instead of fighting them.

We already use Slack, QuickBooks, and Google Calendar. Do we have to switch?

No. Slack and Teams mirror into the same conversations, Gmail and Outlook are managed without leaving, calendars unify into one view, and QuickBooks and Xero stay in sync. Consolidation happens on your schedule — the platform meets your tools where they are, and the knowledge stops living in ten places.

What happens to my existing data?

It comes with you. Your existing records — customers, projects, documents, history — are brought into the platform as part of setup, organized under your structure and terminology. You don't start from a blank system, and you don't maintain the old ones in parallel.

Do I need IT staff to run it?

No. ReCaptured is delivered and shaped for you — and when your needs change, we change the platform with them. New fields, workflows, and modules are handled as part of the service, without a development project or a technical hire on your side.

Who can see my data, and how is it protected?

Your data is yours, and access to it is deliberate. Role-based permissions control who sees what, every change carries a full audit history, and data handling is disciplined to the same control requirements behind frameworks like SOC 2 and GDPR. If you have specific data-handling requirements, bring them to the discovery call — walking through them is exactly what it's for.

What does the AI do without asking me?

Nothing that changes your records. The AI drafts, suggests, and surfaces — then waits for your confirmation before anything is committed. Every action it takes is tracked with full history: what changed, when, and on whose approval.

What if my industry isn't listed?

The industries above are examples, not a menu. The platform is shaped to each business it runs — whatever its work, workflows, and vocabulary — and any operating business can be mapped in a discovery session. If your business has customers, work, and knowledge worth keeping, it fits.

How fast can we be up and running?

Days. A 30-minute discovery call maps your business; the workspace is then shaped around your terminology and workflows and delivered ready to use. When new requirements come up after launch, they’re handled the same way — shaped into your workspace in days, not months.
