AI assistant

An AI assistant that only knows what your business knows.

The assistant inside ReCaptured belongs to your organization, answers from your records, and never acts on its own. This page lists capabilities by how far along they are, and we update it with every release rather than promise dates.

Available now Designed, after launch

Available now

  • Answer from your records. Ask about a client, a project or a deal; it answers from what your team has shared and cites the record.
  • Follow the screen you're on. Its context follows the section you're in: a deal, a project, a person.
  • Draft tasks from transcripts. Meetings become drafted commitments, each anchored to the minute it was made.
  • Triage intake. Suggests owner, project, priority and duplicates on every new item, with its reasoning shown. One click to accept.
  • Guard deadlines. Watches SLA clocks, notice windows, expiring documents and review cycles; escalates on a policy ladder before anything is forfeited.
  • Propose rebalances. Capacity math plus who actually closes what, with proposed moves you apply or decline.
  • Capture decisions from chat. Spots a decision or commitment in a thread and offers to file it to the record, with provenance.
  • Draft replies and briefs. Follow-up emails, call briefs and status digests, drafted for a person to edit and send.
  • Work from the assistant you already use. Connect ChatGPT or Claude to your ReCaptured records through MCP. An admin turns it on per organization and chooses which tools are exposed.
  • Enrichment. Looks up company facts from public and connected sources and offers candidates with their source; you choose what gets written, and a PII rule blocks anything it shouldn't.
  • Organization vocabulary. Tell it how your business talks (house terms, tone, what never to abbreviate) and it drafts that way.

Designed, after launch

  • Answer the phone. A voice front door that qualifies inbound callers, texts back missed calls and books time on your calendar, with a person confirming the booking.
  • Daily field brief. Turns the day's field recordings into one reviewed summary for the morning huddle. Arrives with Field Audio.
  • Build your own automations in plain English. "No lead goes three days without a follow-up." Typed steps, approval gates, and a run log that names the exact version that ran.
  • Expenses. Receipts by photo or email, read and routed for approval.
  • Invoicing and accounting. Pay apps, lien waivers, QuickBooks and Xero sync.
  • Drawings to takeoff. Upload plans; get a bill of materials.
  • Industry packs. Marketing agencies, law firms, event planning, boat clubs on the same engine.

How we keep this page honest

Two tiers, no dates. If something is listed under "Available now", it is in the product your team signs into on day one. If it's under "Designed, after launch", we'll move it up when it ships, not before. We don't run a beta list on this site; pilot partners see new features first and hear about them directly.

How the assistant behaves, on every plan

1
Confirm-first is the hard ceiling.Anything leaving your organization, an email, a notice, an e-sign request, waits for a person. No setting removes this.
2
Nothing lands without review.Drafted tasks, captured decisions and proposed changes show what will happen, with which arguments, and who can approve.
3
Private and Off-the-Record are never indexed.Direct messages are private by structure. Admins can set a channel to never index, or to capture signal only.
4
Every answer carries provenance.A record, a transcript minute, a file version. If it can't cite it, it doesn't say it.
5
It sees what you can see.It answers scoped to the reader's permissions. A field role sees field records; a guest sees their project.
6
Every call is metered and logged.Which model ran, what it drew from your allowance, and what it touched. Fields it has edited carry a small marker.

The assistant started with one rule: notice what's missing before anyone has to ask. The follow-up nobody sent. The decision nobody wrote down. That's still its job.
